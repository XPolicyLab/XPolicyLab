### Procedures

Solve the full current folding instruction from reset using RGB/calibration/proprioception and tools. do not waste actions; no voluntary finish or per-cell/request quota. Every recoverable issue returns to you; never replay a partly executed cell. Only episode end or preserved external interruption ends the episode.
Read API.md in this namespace once. from library.fold_clothes import cloth_skills as cloth; m=cloth.new_memory(). The module is optional bounded tools, not an autonomous full policy. Prefer docstrings; do not repeatedly dump its32000bytes. Direct public primitives are allowed when the helper contract does not suit the current cloth state. Do not spend the episode satisfying a tracker that cannot see an occluded/textureless patch: acquire a useful view, reselect a real feature, or switch to a simpler visually grounded action.
Plan sleeve folds, paired hem fold, and final check before manipulation. Track material identities: both cuffs, opposite chest regions, shoulder edge and both hem corners. Use images, overlays/crops and calibrated correspondences; never invent a precise3D target or assume a rigid object model for cloth. A short lift must show cloth following before transport; gripper aperture alone is not proof.
A useful strategy is: acquire both cuffs and keep the other side stabilized while folding each sleeve inward toward the opposite chest. This is a candidate, not a mandatory strategy. Avoid excessive tension that moves the torso. Releasing one side may move the other; inspect after release and after a short settling interval. Keep folds well inside the visible torso, with margin against relaxation rather than exactly on a boundary.
For the lower fold, grasp inside both visible hem corners, verify both grips, and carry them together toward corresponding shoulders, preserving the edge direction. A low controlled arc with supported laydown is a candidate. Avoid high throws or unilateral corrections that undo the other corner. Observe actual cloth: robot pose and successful IK do not prove folded geometry. If a sleeve escapes beneath the hem, controlled partial unfolding, sleeve repair and refolding is allowed. Bounded regrasping is preferable to blind repeated identical commands.
Before release verify cloth supported. Keep fingers open long enough to disengage, then withdraw empty jaws upward/backward without dragging the cloth. After settling inspect both sleeves and the two folded-edge corners, use another camera if needed. Sequential or synchronized release may be appropriate depending on tension; preserve the other held corner until supported.
IMPORTANT: home only once, at the very end, when the folded geometry is right. During manipulation use safe non-home parking poses in open space instead. Do not use home as a routine viewpoint reset or a test of whether the task is complete. Only after the visual final checklist passes, release appropriately and home both arms. The episode end may arrive during the motion; stop dispatch then.
do not waste actions. Spend model decisions at uncertain grasp/contact/release transitions; group read-only inspections and validated bounded movement internally. Track the checklist.

SLEEVE STABILITY LESSON: A cuff lying inside the garment silhouette is not enough. Folding the opposite sleeve can drag the first cuff outward even after its initial release looked good. Before grasping either hem, explicitly find both actual cuff ends again using close head/wrist views and marked image overlays. Track the original cuff material, not a nearby shoulder seam or a fold ridge. Aim each cuff across the torso midline toward the opposite chest, with generous inward overlap; do not stop at the near-side torso edge. Use the currently observed chest/torso frame rather than a remembered world target. After the second sleeve is released and both hands clear, recheck and, if needed, regrasp the first cuff and lay it farther across. This check occurs BEFORE the hem hides the cuffs. If the cuff is occluded, get a useful oblique view or gently expose it rather than declaring the silhouette sufficient. If a single pass leaves a bulky high loop that springs back, a supported low laydown and small smoothing/regrasp are alternatives. Preserve the successful other fold and leave room to settle. A before/after overlay should show where both cuff ends landed relative to the current torso. Compact overall appearance is not evidence of both cuffs being tucked correctly.
