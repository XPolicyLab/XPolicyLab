# Conditional toast lessons

## Object/scene geometry

### Conditional perception and geometry observations

- On an oblique bread view, mistaking a bottom-edge point for the far corner produced over 20 mm stereo disagreement despite plausible slot checks. Re-identifying the physical corner reduced disagreement below 2 mm. Validate corner correspondence residuals independently of the insertion gate.
- A finger-occluded bottom corner was mistaken for a visible edge endpoint, shortening the measured outline enough to pass the gate while bread caught the front rim. Check the complete face extent; a low stereo residual at a visible edge point does not identify a hidden corner.
- A near-frontal wrist fit accepted low reprojection error yet produced strongly unequal slot widths inconsistent with RGB. An elevated, centered view of both divider edges resolved it; acceptance alone did not validate feature identities.
- A square-template bread pose passed insertion gates but its release caught the rim. Replacing assumed face dimensions with calibrated texture stereo supported seated releases. Validate metric face geometry independently before relying on a template-based depth estimate.
- With only a narrow bread-face strip visible, low-error single-view texture fits changed inferred lean by several degrees without visible motion and disagreed with clearance checks. This was pose ambiguity, not confirmed physical slip; current second-view edge observations distinguished it.
- Sequential wrist texture stereo while the loaded idle arm drifted inferred a nearly horizontal face despite visibly upright bread. The fit was rejected. Check scene rigidity as well as feature residuals before using temporal stereo; low pixel error alone does not establish a stationary bread plane.
- With matching bread textures visible in both slots, unrestricted feature matches selected the wrong slice and implied a false flat pose. Restricting each view to the intended visible slice restored consistent stereo geometry; both gated releases and task completion succeeded.
- When stereo calibration forces a positive-x normal, a right-side observed bread face needs both normal and plane offset reversed before shifting to its midplane. This inward correction, fresh tracking and settled gated releases supported both placements at task completion.

## Grasping/contact/other

`--self-play mode`

### general strategy

- Use current RGB and camera geometry to localize bread, rack, slots and lever. Begin each attempt with inherited guidance, re-localize all targets, and never replay old world coordinates.
- Measure the empty toaster slots with accepted fit_toaster_slots results before pickup; preserve the fit only while the toaster stays still. Choose an exposed slice and a reachable pickup, lift and carry route. Use bread_opening with observed thickness and finger-body clearance.
- Inspect the reached approach, close only with pads at the intended contact, and verify a short visible lift before carry. Preserve successful stages; after failure change one diagnosed element and record the observation that motivates it.
- Measure the actual held bread after carry or contact. Align its projected outline to the slot; a reached TCP or commanded orientation does not establish bread alignment. Preserve a viable inclined grasp rather than leveling or handing off without need.
- Before release, settle the closed grasp using holding actions, remeasure the bread, and require a fresh accepted check_bread_insertion. Use release_bread, inspect support and finger clearance before retreat, then verify both slots before pressing the lever.
- Complete the full task: two slices in the slots, the remaining bread undisturbed in the rack, lever down beyond its required travel, and both arms home. Verify after withdrawal and homing. A successful intermediate stage is not full task success.

### grasping failures

- Closing after a bread descent stopped above the intended contact produced empty grasps. Inspect actual pad alignment and blockage before closing; withdraw and remeasure a slice displaced by contact.
- Repeated narrow approaches to crowded upright bread remained obstructed; an exposed end slice with clearance for both complete finger bodies allowed pickup. Opening must reflect slice thickness, closing direction and neighboring bread.
- A thin corner hold rotated or slipped during lift. Check the first short lift from images and confirm the selected slice clears the rack before transport; a holding flag or small nonzero opening alone did not establish retention.
- A blocked rack approach changed gripper orientation, so a recovery that omitted roll inherited unwanted rotation. Retract, inspect actual orientation and specify the intended full pose before the next approach.
- A low bread hold left the fingers against the toaster housing before the slice could enter. Consider how the chosen contact height leaves the lower bread exposed for insertion; an aligned TCP did not establish seating.
- An oblique front-edge grip progressively rotated an outer slice as grip width narrowed, then lost it during a stationary hold. Short lifts alone did not validate retention. Treat continuing bread-only rotation as an abort cue while original rack support remains reachable.
- An empty cross-table pickup approach collided with the homed idle arm and displaced it. Withdrawing the working arm and parking the idle arm outboard cleared the same approach. The subsequent bread closure was empty, so the cleared route did not validate a grasp.
- An upright displaced slice leaned on lower-edge support. A 45-degree overhead approach contacted its broad face before pad engagement, changed its support, and subsequent withdrawal left it flat on the table. A narrow-side yaw alone did not account for the leaning face along the finger approach.

### recovery when bread lies flat on the table

- If a slice falls flat on the tabletop, try a coordinated two-hand pickup before repeating single-hand edge pinches. Re-observe the slice and choose contacts from its current pose; the earlier upright pickup pose no longer applies.
- Use one hand to stabilize the slice against sliding while the other gently grips and raises an accessible edge. If that exposes a better contact, reposition the supporting hand to grasp the raised edge or broad faces. Keep support until the receiving hand visibly retains the slice through a short test lift.
- Make small, sequential movements and inspect bread retention and finger separation between them. Keep both hands clear of the table and each other; do not pull them apart while both grips constrain the bread. If the edge cannot be reached with table clearance, change the approach rather than pushing the slice toward the table edge or forcing a lower grasp.

### carry failures

- A pickup chosen without a reachable loaded lift and destination approach forced difficult recovery or handoff. Choose the arm and grasp with the complete transfer in mind; one failed correction pose does not prove handoff is necessary.
- A receiving descent through the donor fingers displaced the donor and destabilized the slice. Keep the donor fixed, establish clear exposed contact and inspect small receiver approach steps before transferring the hold.
- The slice rotated within the fingers during carry or yaw adjustment; inserting from its pickup orientation then misaligned it. Measure the current bread plane and outline after transport and after any contact or visible shift.
- Moving an observing arm into the workspace of the arm holding bread caused interference. Choose a clear view with the holding arm stationary and inspect actual arm separation before moving the observer.
- A grip reading nearly twice the RGB-measured slice thickness retained a short lift but later slipped during pitch/yaw correction. Treat that mismatch as an unstable-contact cue, not a confirmed thickness measurement.
- During a receiving closure, simultaneous widening of the still-closed donor and a receiver width near twice the bread thickness exposed handoff interference. Reopening and withdrawing only the receiver restored donor width without losing bread; the donor was not released.
- A steep receiving grasp at an interior upper contact briefly matched slice thickness after donor opening, but lost retention during donor withdrawal. Moving the contact away from the far corner did not by itself fix the handoff; this failed variant is not a retained-transfer recipe.

### placement failures

- Opening after a failed placement motion dropped bread onto the table. Retain the grasp after IK failure or blockage, withdraw clear, and re-observe before choosing a changed approach.
- Release from approximate slot geometry or an assumed held-bread offset left slices across the rim. Check the actual bread outline against a fresh accepted slot fit and insertion check; a reached gripper target did not prove support.
- Continuing a lever press after housing or arm interference did not establish full travel. Disengage, inspect the lever track and change contact or approach geometry before retrying; verify the lever after the hand withdraws.
- do not waste actions on extra lever presses or unnecessary staging. Complete disengagement, both-arm homing and final checks; inspect Home.reached.
- After a shallow 30-degree lever press, 25 mm rear withdrawal left the lever down, but diagonal lifting and homing were obstructed near the housing. A subsequent lateral move cleared the fingers; full homing remained unverified at the attempt limit.
- Repeated closed-grasp holds of a loaded cross-table arm showed about 1–2 mm lateral hand drift per hold, followed by bread slip. Both aligned slices later settled into slots, but this did not validate controlled release; distinguish whole-hand drift from bread-only slip.
- A housing-limited descent widened a thickness-matching bread grip; a subsequent closed retreat lost the slice into the slot. For the other slice, a short lift restored grip width before re-centering and gated release. These observations do not validate the uncontrolled first placement.
- After a centered overhead grip retained carry, replacing 30 mm descents with 5 mm steps retained the slice initially but did not prevent growing cross-slot lean and grip narrowing. A corrective rotation then lost the grasp into the slot. Small descents alone were not a validated retention fix.
- A housing-blocked front descent closed empty while bread stayed upright on the fingers within the slot. A 0.3 opening did not clear it; a fresh gated 0.5 opening and staged rear withdrawal allowed seating. Empty closure alone was not a flat-table drop.
