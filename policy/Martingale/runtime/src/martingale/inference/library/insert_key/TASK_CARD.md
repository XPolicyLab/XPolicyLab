# insert_key task card

One attempt from the original scene: pick up the key, hand it over if needed, stand it upright over the keyhole,
insert it fully, then turn it clockwise. `insert_key/EPISODE_LESSON.md`
gives the tool sequence.

**Physical strategy.**
- The key is most easily dropped at the first grasp, the lift and the hand-over, and every recovery eats the
  actions needed for insertion and turning. Prevent drops from the first grasp: a shaft pinch beside the bow to
  lift, then a deep grip on the bow's flat faces with full pad contact for everything that rotates the key.
- A short lift, a closed gripper or a holding flag does not prove a grip that resists rotation. Look at how the
  key sits between the pads after each loaded motion; a key that turns by itself in the grip is slipping, even if
  it now looks more upright.
- Keep the holding hand still while the other hand approaches, closes and is checked; release only after that.
- Rotate a held key in clear space, well above the lock; near the lock use small translations and small tilt
  corrections, each followed by a fresh look.

**do not waste actions.** Plan the whole route (pickup, hand-over, upright pose, insertion and the turned pose, all reachable
by the chosen arms) before spending actions, including alignment, insertion and the turn. Save actions
by reusing measurements while nothing moved, by combining an empty hand's opening with its travel, and by
avoiding camera tours, detours, large presentation rotations and repeated blocked commands; never by skipping a
retention check or by speeding up a weak grip.

**Judge from images, calibration and proprioception.** There is no score during the episode.
- Upright: the shaft looks vertical in two views taken from different sides.
- Centred: the tip is over the round part of the keyhole, and the teeth are in line with the slot.
- Fully in: nothing of the shaft or teeth shows above the lock face in a side view, the bow sits on or just above
  the face, and a further short feed gains almost nothing.
- Turned: the bow has been rotated clockwise, as viewed from above.
  orientation, with the key still fully in and upright. Do not withdraw it afterwards.
Missing or stale estimates are unknown, not a pass. Partial stages are not task success.
