## Reusable execution choices

1. Resolve the current language target and inspect grasp geometry in head and wrist views. Choose a solid component with opposing surfaces that fits the jaw axis, not the object's projected center, a cavity, or a visible region without contact clearance. Re-localize every episode.
2. Select orientation with the whole approach and lift in mind. Tilted down30 helped central reach for these objects; a vertical pinch worked on the scissors. Neither is universal. Do not rotate while holding unless necessary. Do not append dependent relative motions after a marginal reach: a failed command can leave a different origin while the remaining batch continues.
3. Descend to the chosen contact region, not to a universal height above the table. The wrist cross indicates projection, not depth/contact. Tilted-view parallax and palm clearance matter. If descent stalls, inspect and correct lateral clearance; do not force the same descent deeper. Headphone correction succeeded once, while deeper owl contact pushed the object and shallower contact later slipped.
4. Inspect closure, then confirm retention during the required lift. Opening above the empty baseline (~0.18) is only a candidate grasp. The owl's 0.69 false-positive shows why opening alone is insufficient. A short first portion of the required lift may reveal slippage earlier; do not add an unnecessary lift-lower cycle. A better owl contact/orientation remains to be tested; do not simply repeat either failed height.
5. Budget the full sequence before moving. Observed closure cost varies from 13 to 34 steps; movement can exceed the generic prompt estimate. Provisionally reserve about 60 steps for close/settling and lift, while avoiding extra observations that cause unnecessary motions. This is a planning heuristic, not a hard simulator boundary. After an empty/slipping grasp, retry only if reopening, realigning, closing and lifting all fit the remaining native budget.
6. Native completion can interrupt a requested move: the headphone lift reported a partial target because success terminated execution after sufficient rise. Check the native result before calling that a motion failure. Conversely, a completed robot lift with the object left behind is failure. Do not reset or place if the instruction only asks to lift.
